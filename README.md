Artefact 1: eSafety Commissioner vs X – Online Safety and Freedom of Expression




Summary of the Artefact




The ABC News article covers an argument between Australia's eSafety Commissioner and X over a video of a man being
stabbed while attending a church in Sydney. The eSafety Commissioner wanted the content removed from the site, but
X disputed the Commissioner's request. The battle brought up the issue of the extent to which the Australian Regulator
can reach a global social media service. The Federal Court was engaged and the eSafety Commissioner subsequently
dropped the case. The case illustrates the challenges that can occur in implementing cross-border regulation of
harmful material on the internet.










Justification for Choosing this Artefact





This artefact was selected as it was an immediate example of government regulation of online content in an Australian
context. It is particularly significant for Week 9 workshop due to the eSafety Commissioner versus X case study presented
in the workshop as well as highlighting the tensions between national regulation and global platforms. The artefact also
illustrates how governments, courts and tech companies can all make decisions on censorship. This is meaningful evidence
of my learning because I can use the online safety, freedom of expression, government authority and the global nature of
the internet concepts that I learnt in the workshop to a real case.








Artefact 2: Nepal Social Media Ban and the 2025 ‘Gen Z’ Protests






Summary of the Artefact






Amnesty International’s 2025 research briefing looks at the events that happened around Nepal’s social media restrictions
and the Gen Z protests in September 2025. The report tells that the government had asked media platforms to register with
the Ministry of Communication and Information Technology. When platforms did not meet the registration requirement
authorities put a ban on services on 4 September, which blocked 26 platforms, including Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp,
YouTube, LinkedIn, Reddit, Signal and Snapchat. The report says that these restrictions sparked widespread anger and
protesters used VPNs and encrypted applications to keep talking to each other. It also records the crackdown, on protesters
and looks at the human rights concerns that came from that response.

the relationship between government regulation, censorship, freedom of expression, technical circumvention and social
consequences. The report notes that protesters used VPNs and encrypted apps which connects with the workshop’s question,
about how effective censorship can be when users have technologies that can bypass restrictions.








Artefact 3: Australia’s Under-16 Social Media Restrictions

Summary of the Artefact






The Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024 made changes to Australia's online safety framework,
which included introducing minimum-age provisions for some social media services. This law mandates that social media
operators of the age-restricted platforms "take measures that are reasonable to ensure that children under the minimum
age cannot obtain an account on the platform. Requirements began on 10th December, 2025. The law thus imposes duties on
online platforms to create and run processes that meet the law. It also poses practical challenges of age verification,
privacy, security and access to on-line communication.









Justification for Choosing this Artefact






I chose this artefact because it is a current Australian example of government regulation of social media access.
It directly relates to the Week 9 workshop, which includes Australia’s under-16s Social Media Ban as a 2026 case
study and asks when protecting society online can become a restriction on freedom. The artefact demonstrates that
government regulation can take different forms and does not always involve blocking an entire website. It is meaningful
evidence of learning because it connects the legal regulation of social media with ICT issues such as privacy, security,
age-assurance technology and the responsibilities of technology providers.











Artefact 4: Freedom on the Net 2024 – Internet Censorship in China








Summary of the Artefact









The Freedom House country report focuses on internet freedom in China between 1 June 2023 and 31 May 2024.
It states that there are significant limitations on access to and content available on the Internet, such as
blocking access to foreign Internet sites and social media services, obligating domestic technology companies
to monitor and delete banned content, and limiting access to VPNs not officially authorised by the government.
According to the report, the ‘Great Firewall' is a huge censorship tool and that by the end of the reporting period,
GFWatch identified more than 200,000 blocked domains. It also references the banning of political criticism, accounts
on the internet and information about protests, and pressure on technology companies to meet government mandates.
